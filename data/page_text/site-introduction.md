### Reducing the Vulnerability of the State Water Project to Climate Change

Over the next 20 years, California could lose 10 percent of its overall water supplies because of climate change. Delivery capability and reliability of the State Water Project could be reduced as much as 23 percent. The State Water Project (SWP) delivers water to public agencies serving 27 million Californians.

The [SWP Adaptation Strategy](<https://cadwr.app.box.com/s/yfrzk3xxwic3xxiljhl2qqh6m4l7ymth>) describes more than a dozen approaches the SWP is pursuing to address the effects of climate change. Five of these strategies are quantitatively evaluated to show how they could be expected to improve SWP reliability in the future.  

This work builds on the [SWP Delivery Capability Report (DCR)](<https://water.ca.gov/Library/Modeling-and-Analysis/Central-Valley-models-and-tools/CalSim-3/DCR>), which provides a biennial assessment of how much water the SWP can reliably deliver under current and future conditions. While the DCR illustrates the potential challenges posed by climate change, the Adaptation Strategy extends the analysis by exploring potential solutions.  

This is the SWP Adaptation Strategy **Results Console (ReCon)**, an interactive dashboard designed to complement the Adaptation Strategy main report. ReCon presents outputs from [CalSim 3](<https://water.ca.gov/Library/Modeling-and-Analysis/Central-Valley-models-and-tools/CalSim-3>)—the operations model used in this analysis—and enables users to explore technical details and scenario outcomes beyond what is summarized in the published reports.
